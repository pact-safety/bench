Weekly publication on the political economy of AI — the structural,
governance, and economic-incentive layer that sits *above* the
technical capability frontier. Adjacent to (but distinct from) the
classic AI-alignment forum vein: less "is the model deceptive,"
more "what coordination problem does this technology create
between labs / states / publics."

**Why read.** Roles at safety / governance orgs (GovAI, AISI,
BlueDot CTG cohorts) interview on more than evals and interp —
they want signal on whether you can think about the policy /
political layer with the same rigor you bring to a training loop.
This is one of the better current sources for that — current,
weekly, and engaging the political-economy literature seriously.

**What to take from it.**

1. **The structural framing.** Read 2-3 recent posts and notice the
   pattern: identify the actors, identify the incentive gradients,
   identify where leverage is. This is the reasoning shape that
   governance interviews probe.
2. **Counterarguments.** Leicht engages with positions he disagrees
   with rather than dismissing them. That's the move; if you can
   reconstruct *the strongest version of the position you reject*
   in a writing sample, that signals seriously.
3. **Specific recent debates.** Whatever's on the front page when
   you read it — current discourse evolves fast.

**Pair with** the [Hamming foundations](/content/r11-hamming-foundations)
reading for the "how to think about important problems" frame; pair
with the [reflection / Hamming mode](/reflect) on this app, which
is the same prompt class applied to your own work.

**Strategy.** Background-read fine; this is for breadth + framing
practice, not memorization. Skim the latest post, pick one whose
question interests you, read it deeply.
