Welcome to Li's Bench

A daily drill tool calibrated for the actual selection surface of research-engineer roles at frontier labs, safety orgs, and fellowships — paper-reproduction, debugging real ML code, the occasional algo problem. Not generic FAANG SWE prep.

Onboarding here is about collecting a useful starting signal. If you feel uncertain on a prompt, choose the uncertain option — it helps record your starting point and does not affect an application outcome. These answers do contribute to your starting scores.

How a problem flows here

Every core problem is structured around the five phases — a rhythm for solving under uncertainty:

  1. Explore — pin down the I/O, constraints, edge cases.
  2. Brainstorm — two or three approaches, with trade-offs.
  3. Plan — a paragraph-deep outline. The strongest single signal in interviews.
  4. Implement — write the code. Editor unlocks after the Plan section has content.
  5. Verify — properties, boundary cases, hand-traced inputs.

Warmups skip the scaffolding — they're intentionally low-ceremony.

Pick your starting point

Choose a curriculum, start with an assessment, or try an easy problem. A curriculum gives you a sequence to follow; the assessment helps pick a starting problem; easy problems let you begin practicing right away.

Take the assessment

Up to ten multiple-choice questions, selected for topic coverage. This brief snapshot does not cover every skill axis or measure interview readiness. It seeds your starting profile and suggests a first problem from the assessed axes. Retake any time from Settings.

Skip — ramp up with easy problems

The scheduler biases toward easy problems for your first 10 attempts, then graduates to mediums and hards as it learns from your confidence ratings and pass/fail data. The assessment is always available later if you want richer signal sooner.

Choose a curriculum

Browse tracks, see what each starts with, and choose one for your current goal. Work in short sessions and return to your saved progress. You can take the assessment later.
