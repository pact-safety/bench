One place to learn technical AI safety

bench pulls the field into one daily practice: foundational papers, exercises, coding problems, curricula like ARENA and ML4Good, the week's news, and timed mock interviews. It looks at where you are weak and at the role you're aiming for, and picks what you work on next.

bench is part of PACT, which builds open-source tools so independent researchers can evaluate AI systems for themselves. The code is at github.com/pact-safety/bench.

Why it exists

The people who can build evals, reproduce papers, and read model internals are mostly trained inside frontier labs. The groups that check those labs need the same skills: safety institutes, independent evaluators, auditors, and academic teams. They hire from a small pool.

bench makes those skills cheaper to learn outside a lab. It's free to self-host and works with your own API key. The practice targets what these roles actually test.

Every core problem runs in five phases, and the editor stays locked until you've written a plan.

  1. Explore pin down inputs, outputs, constraints, edge cases
  2. Brainstorm two or three approaches and their trade-offs
  3. Plan a paragraph-deep outline
  4. Implement write the code
  5. Verify properties, boundary cases, hand-traced inputs

For researchers

Aim at a real role, then practice toward it

A short assessment maps you across {{ stats.axes }} skill axes. Paste a job posting and bench reads what the role needs, then shifts your daily mix toward the gaps.

  • The whole field in one queue. Papers, videos, exercises, and problems are tagged on the same axes and scheduled together, so reading feeds practice.
  • Mock interviews. Interview mode hides hints and reference solutions and runs the clock, so you practice the conditions you'll face.
  • Feedback on your process. Review looks at each phase, not only whether the tests pass.
  • Retrieval on a schedule. Explain a concept back in your own words. What you miss returns in a day; what holds returns in a week, then a month.

For programs

Your curriculum, with retention attached

Cohort programs deliver material well and rarely know what stuck. bench runs your sequence as a track and adds practice and retrieval underneath it.

  • Tracks follow your syllabus. {{ stats.tracks }} ship today, including ARENA and ML4Good Technical Prework. A track is a manifest file, so adding yours doesn't need a rebuild.
  • Practice away from the notebook. Finishing a notebook with autocomplete on doesn't prove the idea landed. bench asks for it again later, without the cell that produced it.
  • Each learner's data is their own. The hosted instance scopes every learner's records to that learner.
  • Early enough to shape. Cohort reporting isn't built yet. If you run a program, now is when it's cheap to build it around how yours works.
