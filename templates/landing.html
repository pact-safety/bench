Practice that behaves like the work

Bench is a daily drill environment for people moving into AI safety research engineering. Not flashcards, and not FAANG interview prep — problems with the shape of real research work, a mentor that pushes back, and a schedule that decides what you see next.

The gap this closes

Most preparation optimises for recall on demand. Research engineering does not work like that: you are handed an underspecified problem, you have to find the constraints yourself, choose between approaches that are all defensible, and then be honest about whether it worked. The skills that decide the outcome — framing, trade-off reasoning, debugging your own reasoning — are exactly the ones a problem set with an answer key never exercises.

Bench structures every core problem around five phases, and the editor stays locked until the plan has content in it. You cannot skip to typing.

  1. Explore pin down the I/O, constraints, edge cases
  2. Brainstorm two or three approaches, with trade-offs
  3. Plan a paragraph-deep outline
  4. Implement write the code
  5. Verify properties, boundary cases, hand-traced inputs

For researchers

You are trying to do the work, not pass a quiz

Bench picks what you do each day from where you are actually weak, across {{ stats.axes }} skill axes — transformer internals, training mechanics, eval design, numerical stability, debugging, and interpretability and safety fundamentals alongside them.

  • A mentor that disagrees with you. Mira is short, specific and honest, and pushes back on a weak experiment design rather than congratulating you for having one.
  • Retrieval on a schedule, not a streak. Say a concept back in your own words; what you miss returns in a day, what holds returns in a week, then a month.
  • No score, ever. A miss is scheduling information, not a grade — the app tells you what comes back sooner. There is no percentage, no streak to break, and no line that goes down.
  • Bring your own material. Papers, lectures and exercises you are already working through get scored on the same axes and enter the same daily rotation.

For programs

Your curriculum, with retention attached

Cohort programmes are good at delivering material and structurally bad at knowing what stuck. Bench runs a curriculum as a track — the sequence you already teach — and puts a retrieval loop underneath it.

  • Tracks carry your sequence. {{ stats.tracks }} ship today, including ARENA 3.0 and MATS-oriented paths; a track is a manifest, so yours is a file rather than a rebuild.
  • Closes the notebook gap. Working through a notebook with autocomplete on teaches your editor, not you. Bench asks for the same idea cold, later, away from the cell that produced it.
  • Per-learner data stays per-learner. Row-level security is on every table, scoped to the owner.
  • Early enough to shape. Cohort-level reporting is not built yet. If you run a programme and want it built the way your programme actually works, now is when that is cheap.
